1. Behavioral pricing concepts (5 points)

Full credit requires that the response:

  • identifies two of the five major, overarching behavioral pricing principles covered in class, named and described accurately; and
  • explains how at least one of them could be used to improve the chosen company’s plan-selection page.

Note: AI tools do not know which five principles this course emphasizes, so this answer must come from your own recall of the course material.

2. Ideas from lecture and from competitors (5 points)

Several of your Step 1 answers together make up these 5 points; they are not scored separately.

  • Discusses multiple ideas drawn from the class examples and from the competitor or “ahead-of-the-curve” company.
  • For each idea, explains whether it would make sense to apply in this company’s context and why (for example, its customers, price level, or product).

3. Positioning and page differences (5 points)

  • Describes the brand positioning of the chosen company and of the competitor.
  • Identifies at least two differences in their positioning and, for each, at least one way the difference is communicated to customers.
  • Cites specific details from the pricing pages that differ, such as the number of plans, the prices of the plans, and how features or prices are presented.

4. Redesign planning: questions B1–B10 (10 points)

  • Answers every part of each of the ten questions, including B10, the complete list of changes.
  • Explains not only what you would do but why, with reasoning that draws on relevant concepts or examples from class and on the specific company’s context.
  • B10 lists at least three changes, at least two of which are pricing-related (they change prices or perceived price/value). The changes must be well reasoned and substantive, not three minimal tweaks.
  • B10 is written clearly enough that an AI tool could easily make the intended changes from your description alone, without guessing or asking follow-up questions.

5. The webpages and redesign (20 points)

This category covers the live pages you build in Step 3. Full credit requires that:

  • before.html is a faithful, working replica of the company’s current plan-selection page.
  • after.html implements every change listed in B10 (at least three, including at least two pricing or perceived price/value changes), and the changes are visible and work in the published page.
  • The changes effectively apply relevant concepts from class, including at least two improvements related to behavioral pricing.
  • The layout and elements look professional and realistic, as if the page has been proofread and edited.
  • The text, elements, and changes are clear, well written, and well designed.
  • The changes would realistically improve outcomes for the firm and/or the experience of visitors.
  • Your review of the initial redesign, your discussion of the AI-suggested ideas (Step 3, parts C2–C3), and your shared Lovable chats show that you iterated and made considered decisions.
  • Both pages include the required class-project disclaimer, contain no real payment fields, and do not display real company logos or brand imagery.

6. Two changes and why they matter (8 points)

  • Presents two legitimate changes and, for each, connects the change to the concept from class most closely related to it, explaining how it would affect visitors’ perceptions and choices.
  • A change counts only if it is actually new. Points are lost if a listed “change” was already present on the original page (for example, claiming strikethrough pricing or $.99 endings as a change when the “before” page already used them).

7. Academic article and interpretation (7 points)

  • The source is an academic journal article.
  • Explains what was done in the study, including its main conclusions and how the researchers reached them.
  • Connects the findings to your own redesign and argument.
  • Cites the article properly (in-text citation and full reference; APA is fine).

Required, but not scored separately

  • The initial brainstorm of changes (Step 1).
